ABOUT

Overview

A personal voice assistant that runs on this Mac. You talk, it answers aloud, and it can look things up for you. It reads and reports. It never sends, edits or deletes anything on its own; the one thing it can write is text at your cursor, when you ask for that. The app itself keeps a short memory file of lasting facts, which you can read and edit.

How it is built

  • Window: a small native macOS app written in Swift, showing a web interface.
  • Server: a local Node.js program with no dependencies, reachable only from this Mac.
  • Brain: Claude, through the Claude Code command-line tool and your own Claude subscription. No API key.
  • Listening: Apple's speech recognition, built into macOS.
  • Speaking: the Mac's own text-to-speech voices.
  • Weather: Open-Meteo, a free service needing no account.
  • Radio: FIP's public streams from Radio France, needing no account.

What it does

  • Voice conversation: hold a key to talk, replies spoken sentence by sentence.
  • Global shortcut: talk from any app; optional writing at your cursor.
  • Opening briefing: date, system, weather, today's calendar and AI news.
  • AI news: a daily report from Hacker News, GitHub and the smol.ai digest, read on screen or summed up aloud, with every day kept.
  • Google Calendar: read-only, through your Claude account.
  • Web search and files: searches the web and reads its own workspace folder.
  • Player: FIP radio and your own songs, from its panel or by asking; it plays quietly while you talk.
  • Mini mode: a small always-on-top core.
  • History: past exchanges, searchable.
  • Memory: a fresh conversation after a pause, with lasting facts carried over in a memory file.
  • Usage: estimated cost and token counts, per day and in total.

Where things go

  • On this Mac: settings, history, usage, memory notes and your context file, in ~/JARVIS.
  • To Anthropic: your questions and what JARVIS reads to answer them.
  • To Apple: your voice, when speech recognition is not on-device.
  • To Open-Meteo: the coordinates of your weather town.
  • To Radio France: requests for the FIP stream and its track details, nothing about you.
  • To Hacker News, GitHub and smol.ai: requests for public pages only, nothing about you.
  • Not built in: anything that sends email, changes your calendar or edits files.

Releases

v0.9.012 November 2026CURRENT
  • Player panel: music now has its own panel beside Weather and System, apart from the assistant. It plays FIP radio, with its twelve stations and the track on air, or your own songs in random order.
  • By voice: ask for FIP, a station, your songs, a pause, the next one or a change of volume, and ask what is playing.
  • Music carries on: it drops to a murmur while you talk or a reply is spoken, then comes back. Stop and Esc no longer end it, and the morning song plays in the panel.
v0.8.012 November 2026
  • AI news: once a day JARVIS gathers a report from Hacker News, new projects and releases on GitHub, and the smol.ai digest. No account or key is needed.
  • In the briefing: a setting chooses between hearing the three top stories and having the full report shown on screen.
  • News view: opened from the header, with a report for every day on record. The reports are plain text files in the news folder of the workspace, so JARVIS can also answer questions about them.
v0.7.012 November 2026
  • Fresh conversations: after 30 minutes without a word, a new conversation starts, so replies stay quick and cheap. The pause is a setting.
  • Memory: when a conversation ends, its lasting facts are noted in a short memory file, given to every new conversation. You can read and edit it from Settings, or switch it off.
v0.6.012 November 2026
  • Usage: a view opened from Settings showing what JARVIS has used this session, today, this month and in total, with a bar per day and the full record by day and by model.
  • Saved: each reply's estimated cost and token counts are kept in the data folder, separately from the conversation history.
  • Spent: the counter on the main screen no longer resets when the window reloads, and opens the Usage view when clicked.
v0.5.012 November 2026
  • About: its own view, opened from the header, with the overview and this list of releases.
  • Characters: choose JARVIS or HAL, which changes the look, name, manner of speaking and preferred voice.
  • HAL: a red theme and an eye-shaped core, with one small surprise if you ask about the pod bay doors.
  • Fixed: a question asked right after starting a new conversation was lost.
v0.4.011 November 2026
  • About: a card in Settings covering how JARVIS is built, what it does and where data goes.
  • Settings layout: cards packed into one to four columns by window width, with no gaps.
  • README: requirements, install steps, structure and limits.
v0.111 November 2026
  • First release: a native macOS window, a local server and a web interface, with Claude as the brain.
  • Voice: Apple speech recognition with hold-to-talk, and spoken replies.
  • Looking things up: weather, read-only Google Calendar and an opening briefing.
  • Also: settings, conversation history and optional integration with other apps.
